### *... a great sense of joy [...] mirroring its melancholic base...*

### Release Notes

**Label:** Sonic Pieces\
**Released:** May 2018\
**Mastered By:** Nils Frahm\
**See Also:** *[A Score for Darling](../2018/33978-erik-k-skodvin-rauelsson-a-score-for-darling.md)* and [*Vora*](../2013/22542-rauelsson-vora.md)\
**If You Like:** Deaf Center, Ben Chatwin, Brambles, and Bersarin Quartett

I want to kick off this writeup by recalling some concepts behind my words. I want to remove the timestamp signifying the release date of a particular album and I want you all, along with me, to stop chasing that *latest release*. Great music just is - without words, without judgement, and certainly, without time. It is suspended in the presence of all the timeless and simply waits for you to hear. So notice now, along with me in this short journey, and celebrate all that it has to say. Here is great music that absolutely needs your new attention, and if you haven't heard it up until right now, then now is even better than before. My job, in sharing the delight, will then be done. Enjoy!

**Sonic Pieces** has always plunged in with immense pleasure to all of our sensory organs (including sight and touch), with an intricate and very personal curation by [**Monique Recknagel**](../2018/38204-monique-recknagel-ill-show-you-mine.md). Every album is hand-picked, beloved, and caressed into the packaging that so befits the music. Look through the catalogue and see why every each release on this Berlin-based label deserves an hungered space upon your shelves. For this 27th catalogue entry from the imprint (I'm not counting the *PATTERN* series), we are gifted with the return of **Raúl Pastor Medall** recording under his **Rauelsson** moniker. His previous appearances on the label include the celebrated [*Vora*](../2013/22542-rauelsson-vora.md) debut from 2013, as well as a more recent collaboration with **Erik K Skodvin** on [*A Score For Darling*](../2018/33978-erik-k-skodvin-rauelsson-a-score-for-darling.md), which both have appeared on these pages in the past. And yet I feel that *Mirall* trumps the two.

> Following the intimate but epic Vora from 2013, Rauelsson is back and in grand form with the utterly captivating Mirall, his second full-length album for Sonic Pieces. The new record continues the lush landscapes of earlier times while moving his sound into a feast of organic beats and textural dynamism.

*Mirall* a 40-minute album that has it all - delicate organic atmospherics, heart-synchronizing rhythmic pulses, synth-filtered cascade of arpeggios, and most of all the melodies and cinematic scenes that tell the story from the soul. When I imagine the evolution of electronic music and its perfect marriage with the classical [which, I suppose actually means that electronic *is* the evolution of the classical, and so it loops upon itself], I picture only a handful of monumental albums... and now, *Mirall* is among those few. Perhaps it all makes sense when you create your art and music in a sort of chrysalis of solitude. "*The producer lives on the Spanish coast in a rather detached way from any online presence, focusing on family life and the real sense of humanity. This feeling vibrates through the sound of Mirall making for a very emotional venture.*"

The album is an absolute marvel to listen to, and I'll be sure to return to *Mirall* time and time again. And... if you listen closely, you can hear the magic of **Nils Frahm**'s studio, who also mixed and polished this jewel. I would even go as far as to guess that some of the instruments came from the space (is that the famous organ that I hear?), along with Rauelsson's tape machines, clarinet, string, and piano. The album concludes with a surprising lullaby interpreted by **Heather Woods Broderick**. This is a beauty to marvel at, especially as it's rotating on your turntable. Highly recommended, if you're a follower of all my words...
