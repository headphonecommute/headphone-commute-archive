### The Language of Music: The Narrative

As Victor Hugo famously said: “*Music expresses that which cannot be put into words and that which cannot remain silent.*” Language, spoken or not, is a tool for communication. It is in the story of others that we recognise our life experiences and connect with something deeper, something profoundly human. But what are we saying? And most importantly, how? Let us first zoom out from the particulars of the "what" and examine the "how," focusing on the narrative, the structure, and the craft of storytelling.

We begin with Jóhann. Perhaps no other composer, past or present, has had so much impact on me, as an individual and a musician, as **Jóhann Jóhannsson**. When I learned of his untimely passing in that dark and dreadful February of 2018, I felt that all of the light and sound had somehow collapsed into a singularity and silenced his music forever. And yet, Jóhann goes on to spread the love with music that he left behind. Perhaps in these opening words, there is already a glimpse of the music's purpose — to transcend time and endure through its legacy. Yet, having met Jóhann on several occasions (in Chicago, New York and Reykjavík), I can tell you that it wasn’t his intention. Jóhann composed to tell a story. This is undoubtedly confirmed by his ability to score so many celebrated films, where soundtracks added yet another dimension to the visuals flickering on the screen. But even before the many accolades and the inevitable Hollywood pressures, Jóhann devised his own narratives for which he then composed the music.
